# Pinterest Strategy — Anywhere Learning

**Rewritten 11 August 2026, grounded in 90 days of real account data (13 May – 11 Aug 2026).**

The previous version of this doc was written in April 2026 from expert consensus, before the account had enough data to test anything. Most of its algorithm framing held up. One of its central recommendations did not. This version keeps what the data confirms, cuts what the data killed, and leads with what actually happened.

If you read nothing else, read this: **one pin format is carrying this account, and video is not it.** Numbered free-checklist pins with age framing drive essentially all the traffic. Everything else is noise. Make more of the one thing that works.

### The four patterns behind them

1. **A number in the title.** 28, 16, 50, 12. Four of the top seven lead with a count. The number is a promise of scope, and it survives Pinterest's truncation because it sits in the first three characters.
2. **"Free" plus a concrete artifact.** Checklist, printable, list, PDF. Not "guide", not "resource". The pins that name a specific downloadable thing convert. The account's best pin does both: `(Free Printable Checklist)`.
3. **Age or grade framing.** "by Age", "1st Grade". Parents search by their kid's age because that is how they think about the problem. The #1 and #2 pins both do this and nothing else in the top ten does it as explicitly.
4. **Naming the thing they want to avoid.** "Without a Curriculum". The second-best pin works because it answers an anxiety, not a topic.

The single highest-performing shape on this account is: **`[Number] [Topic] for Kids by Age (Free Printable Checklist)`**.

## 3. What did not work

### Video pins produced zero traffic

This is the big correction. The April doc told you to make video the default and target 30-40% of output. Ninety days of data says otherwise.

| | Impressions | Engagements | Outbound clicks | Saves |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Video pins | 1,470 | 25 | **0** | **0** |
| Everything else | 17,774 | 356 | 124 | 35 |

Video pins also logged 223 views and 50 minutes of total play time. People watched them. Nobody clicked, and nobody saved.

The April doc based its video-first recommendation on a small early sample of IG cross-posts averaging ~32 views against ~2 for static pins. That view-count gap was real, but views were the wrong metric. On a 90-day window with real volume, video converts to nothing.

**What this means in practice:** stop cross-posting IG video to Pinterest as a traffic strategy. It is not costing you much beyond the auto-post, so it can stay on the Homeschool Day in the Life board as top-of-funnel presence. Just do not count it as work toward the pin target, and do not spend original effort making video specifically for Pinterest. Every hour of pin-making should go to static checklist pins until this number changes.

### Personal essay pins

The long-caption story pins (the 15-years-in-classrooms one, the summer-boredom one) pulled 184 and 74 impressions with 5 and 2 engagements, and no meaningful clicks. They read well on Instagram. On Pinterest, where people are searching rather than scrolling a feed, they have no search surface to rank against.

### Individual activity and product covers

Single-activity guide covers (Privacy & Digital Footprint Map, Build a Museum, Sports Stats Lab, and similar) mostly sit at 60-110 impressions with 1-4 engagements. They are competing against nothing and matching no search. The bundle and list pins outperform them consistently.

### The free guide is missing entirely

The lead magnet at `/free-guide` does not appear anywhere in the top 50 pins by any metric. For an account whose main conversion goal is email capture, that is the largest single gap. It needs pins built in the proven checklist shape, not a product-cover pin.

## 4. What is still true from the old doc (verified August 2026)

These held up against current sources and stay in force:

- **Contextual Originality is real.** Pinterest's AI reads the semantic content of the image. A recolored template or a new filename is not a fresh pin. A genuinely different composition with a different value proposition is.
- **Entity recognition over keyword stuffing.** Write natural sentences that answer a search intent. Pinterest reads meaning, not keyword density.
- **Front-load the first 50-60 characters.** Pinterest truncates the description after that. Primary keyword goes in the first three words of the title and the first line of the description.
- **No hashtags.** Still deprioritized. Keep them out of pin and board descriptions.
- **3-5 fresh pins per day** remains the current consensus for an account this size. The "20+ pins a day" playbook is dead.
- **Idea Pins do not exist.** Retired June 2023. They are just pins.
- **Seasonal content needs a 30-45 day head start.** Back-to-homeschool searches peak late August into September, so that content should already be live.
- **Boards need keyword names and 500-character descriptions** with the primary keyword in the first 50-60 characters.

### One thing that is new since April

Pinterest rolled out **GenAI labelling and user-facing filters** in 2026. Pins detected as AI-generated or AI-modified get an "AI modified" label, and users can opt to see less of that content in certain categories. Your pins are Canva-made, not generated, so this is not a current risk. It does mean real photography of real kids doing real activities is worth more than it was, and anything that reads as AI-generated imagery is worth avoiding.

## 5. Pin titles and descriptions

**Title rules:**
- 40-60 characters is the sweet spot, 100 is the ceiling
- Lead with a number where the content supports one
- Primary keyword in the first three words
- Put the artifact in parentheses at the end: `(Free Printable Checklist)`
- No all-caps, no exclamation stacking

**Description rules:**
- 500 character max, but the first 50-60 are what matter
- Repeat the long-tail keyword phrase in the opening line, in natural language
- Name who it is for and what age
- Say what they get, concretely
- Close with the site
- No hashtags

**Template:**

```
[Primary keyword phrase, matching the title]. [What it is, one sentence, secondary keyword].
[Who it is for and what ages]. [What is actually in it].
[CTA to anywherelearning.co].
```

**Worked example, modelled on the #1 pin:**

> 28 life skills for kids by age, from 6 to 14. A free printable checklist covering cooking, money, planning, repairs, and the everyday skills nobody teaches on purpose. Built for homeschool and worldschool families who want real-world learning without worksheets. Print it once and work through it over the year. Get the free checklist at anywherelearning.co.

## 6. Cadence and mix

**Target: 3 fresh pins per day.** That is roughly 90 in a month. If that is not sustainable, 1 per day of the right kind beats 3 of the wrong kind. Quality per pin is doing the work on this account, not volume.

**Mix, based on what the data supports:**

- **60% numbered checklist and idea-list pins.** The proven shape. Fresh imagery each time.
- **20% grade and age-banded posts.** "Without a curriculum", "by age", specific grades.
- **20% seasonal and anxiety-answering blog pins.** Back-to-homeschool, burnout, deschooling, first day.
- **0% original video made for Pinterest.** Let the IG auto-cross-post continue as background presence, nothing more.

**Board priority.** Pin the bulk of new work to Hands-On Learning Activities for Kids, Homeschool Ideas & Encouragement, and Outdoor Nature Activities for Kids. Life Skills is fine but already saturated at 235 pins. Stop actively feeding Writing & Communication and Entrepreneurship until the rest is compounding, and consider whether Critical Thinking's 133 pins are worth continuing to grow.

**Spacing.** Do not pin more than one variation of the same URL in a day. Spread variations across 7-10 days.

## 7. Open items

Both of these were on the April doc and neither has been done. Both are still worth doing.

1. **Rich Pin validation.** Run each content type through https://developers.pinterest.com/tools/url-debugger/ (homepage, /free-guide, a /shop/ product, a /blog/ post) and confirm "Rich Pin found". Validation applies domain-wide once one URL per content type is accepted.
2. **Two portrait Pinterest OG images, 1000x1500.** The code already points at these paths and falls back to the landscape OG until they exist:

## 8. What to measure next, and when

Check again on **11 November 2026** (the next 90-day window), not weekly. Pinterest is slow and daily checking will only make you change things that need time.

The three numbers that matter:

1. **Outbound clicks.** Baseline 124. If the checklist-heavy strategy is working, this should be the metric that moves first.
2. **Outbound click rate.** Baseline 0.64%. This tells you whether the new pins are better than the old ones, independent of how many you made.
3. **Save rate.** Baseline 0.18%, which is low. Saves are the leading indicator of long-tail distribution. If saves climb, clicks follow a month or two later.

**The test:** if numbered checklist pins with age framing do not beat 0.64% outbound click rate over the next 90 days, the problem is keyword targeting, not format. Go back to Pinterest autocomplete and rebuild the keyword bank from live searches.
