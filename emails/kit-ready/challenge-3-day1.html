The Directions Challenge
Time
about 40 min
You need
Paper & a pen
Who leads
You, together
{{ message_content }}

Good morning. Here’s today.

Your kid draws something on a piece of paper and keeps it hidden from you. A house, a robot, a face, a handful of shapes arranged just so. Nothing fancy.

Then they write you instructions for drawing the same picture. Words only. No showing it, no pointing, no “you know, like the other one.”

Then you sit down and draw exactly what the instructions say. Not what you can tell they meant. What they actually wrote.

Then you put the two drawings side by side.
However they turn out, that’s the activity working
If the two are nothing alike, the lesson is sitting right there on the table and you’ll probably laugh. If they come out close to identical, your kid wrote genuinely clear instructions on the first go, which is hard, and worth saying out loud to them. A good result is not a failed activity.

What’s actually happening: this looks like a drawing game, and it isn’t. Your kid just did forty minutes of procedural writing, the kind that shows up in every set of instructions, every recipe, every how-to they’ll ever write. They only think it’s about the picture. Then let them rewrite the instructions and go again on a fresh sheet. The second attempt is always dramatically better, and that rewrite is the real work.

If your kid is older, raise it. Instructions for building something out of Lego, with the original hidden. Or how to get somewhere you both know without naming a single street.

When you’re done: post the two drawings side by side in the group, matching or not. I want to see both kinds. That’s your first entry in the draw.
