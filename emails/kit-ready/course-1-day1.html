The mistake most of us make

When people hear “real-world learning,” they picture one of three things. A field trip. Skipping the academics. Or one more thing to plan on top of everything else.

It’s none of those. Here’s the definition I use:

Real-world learning is when the task would exist even if school didn’t.

Paying at the till. Planning a Saturday. Fixing the thing that broke. The academics are in there, they just ride along. Your kid does the task because it’s real, and the learning comes with it.

The one-question test
Would this exist if school didn’t?
You can use it on anything. A worksheet about money wouldn’t. Paying for your own snack would.
What it looks like at our house

Not long ago I gave my two twenty dollars each and one job: a celebration dinner for the whole family. They put their money together. The meal had to be gluten free and dairy free, which cuts what you can buy and pushes the price up on almost everything, so planning the menu took real work and a little arguing. In the store it came down to one choice: the better bread or the good chocolate for dessert. Not both. We ended up with a drink, a full meal and a homemade sorbet, and they made all of it. Nobody called any of it school, and that’s exactly why it worked.

Try this today
Run the test on your kid’s week. Think of three things they did. Which ones would exist if school didn’t? Most parents find at least one, and it’s usually something they didn’t count as learning.
